# S1E05 — The Ark
# PROMISE: You will ride out the biggest storm in history inside the safest boat ever built — and learn who shut the door.
# REPEATABLE — "God shut the door Himself!"
# ARC — WANT: Noah wants to obey exactly, plank by plank, while the whole world laughs | NEED: to trust God's timing through decades and forty dark days | TURN: the door closes — from the OUTSIDE | TEAR-BEAT: the dove returns with the olive leaf (~70%)
# CAST — NOAH: Physical — a weathered sturdy old man of great age, long white beard, bald crown with wisps, rope-belted work robes, builder's scarred hands. Personality — walks with God when no one else does (Gen 6:9); obeys exactly (Gen 6:22); patient through decades of mockery; first act on dry ground is worship (Gen 8:20). Aliases — "the ark builder", "the old builder".
# GROUP — NOAH'S FAMILY: his wife and three sons with their wives — eight weathered, kind-faced workers in earth-tone robes and head-wraps, always working together (Gen 7:13).
# PLACE — THE LAMP ROOM: a cozy ancient night-time home interior, warm stone walls, a woven rug, a single glowing clay oil lamp on a low round stone table at center, soft hearth-glow at one side, deep indigo night through one small arched window.
# PROP — THE ARK: a vast rectangular wooden vessel of gopher wood sealed dark with pitch, three decks tall, one window-band beneath its roof and ONE great door in its side (Gen 6:14-16) — the same ship in every shot, never a toy boat with animals on deck.
# NOTE — GOD: never depicted; warm light and the Voice alone.
# NOTE — RESTRAINT: the flood is weather and water, never drowning shown; the world outside is grieved in one hushed line; the camera stays with the safe, the storm heard more than seen.

NARRATOR: [[Palette: iron-grey world, one warm figure. Three-second cold open: rain has not yet fallen in the shot — instead, a single old man stands in a dry golden field holding a hammer, while the sky behind him stacks slow mountains of cloud.]] After Eden, people filled the earth... and so, little ones, did the crouching thing. Hearts grew loud and cruel. But one old man still took the evening walk with God. His name was Noah.
NAOMI: Little ones! The stars are out, the night is soft... so tell me — who lights the lamp?
ELI: WE light the lamp!
PIP: Ooooh... watch it glow!
NAOMI: Then gather close, lamplighters — tonight: rain like the world had never seen, a boat like the world had never built, and a door nobody shut with hands. Sing it with me!
ELI: Your word is a lamp for my feet!
PIP: A light for my path so sweet!
ELI: When the road is dark and long —
PIP: Light the lamp and sing along!
NAOMI: Treasure-box first, little ones, because you'll need it: in Noah's day there was no navy, no harbor, maybe not even rain as we know it — and God asked a six-hundred-year-old shepherd-farmer to build a ship taller than your house and longer than a town street. Nowhere near the sea.
PIP: [excited] A boat with NO sea? Everyone must have—
NAOMI: Laughed, little lamb. For YEARS. Hold that. It matters.
VOICE(atlas): Make yourself an ark of gopher wood. Make rooms in it, and cover it with pitch inside and out. For behold — I will bring a flood of waters upon the earth. But I will establish my covenant with YOU. You shall come into the ark — you, your sons, your wife, and your sons' wives with you.
NARRATOR: [[Years compressed in one great working montage: the ark's dark ribs rising against seasons spinning past; Noah's family hauling timber; Noah pitch-blackened to the elbows; distant onlookers pointing and laughing from the dry hills.]] And Noah did EVERYTHING just as God commanded him. Plank by plank, year after laughing year. The Bible gives him our favorite kind of medal — one sentence: Noah did all that God commanded.
ELI: He just kept building? Even with everyone laughing?
NAOMI: Every morning, Eli. Obedience is mostly not lightning and trumpets — it is picking up the same hammer again. Remember the first promise-light we follow? Faithfulness is how it gets carried.
NARRATOR: [[Wonder, not panic: a river of creatures flowing two by two up the ramp into the great dark door — lions unhurried, sparrows in pairs, the slow and the swift, drawn by something gentler than a rope.]] And when the ark stood finished, the animals came. Nobody herded them, little ones. Two by two, of every kind, they came as if Someone had whispered the way — up the ramp, through the one great door, into the rooms prepared for them.
PIP: [awe] Even the grumpy ones? Even BEES?
NAOMI: Even bees, little lamb. Every kind the Maker meant to keep.
NARRATOR: [[The family enters last; Noah pauses on the ramp, one hand on the doorframe, looking back at the darkening hills; then — with no hand upon it — the great door swings slow and firm, and seats itself shut, sealed in a line of warm light.]] Then Noah and his family went in. And the Bible says something wonderful and enormous in five words: "the LORD shut him in." Not Noah. Not his sons. God shut the door Himself.
ELI: [awe] God... shut the door?
NAOMI: [tender] With His own hand, so to speak, Eli. Because when God keeps you safe, the keeping is HIS job, not yours. The storm could rattle that door all it liked — it was holding against the Hand that closed it.
NARRATOR: [[The storm as sound and streaked darkness: rain hammering the window-band, the ark lifting off its cradle into grey mountains of water; inside — lamplight, straw, families of creatures breathing calm.]] And the rain came. Forty days and forty nights, the fountains of the deep and the windows of heaven. The waters rose above the hills themselves. Outside, the old cruel world went under — a grief God Himself felt first and deepest. But inside the ark: lamplight. Breathing. Safety. The covenant, floating.
NARRATOR: [[Time softening: rain thinning to silver, then silence; the ark adrift on a world of bright water under clearing sky; Noah at the window-band releasing a small white dove into the blue.]] And God REMEMBERED Noah — that is the Bible's own word — and the waters began to go down. Noah opened the window and sent out a dove, to see if the world was coming back.
NARRATOR: [[The tear-beat, unhurried: the dove returning across the gold evening water, wings tired, and in her beak — one fresh green olive leaf; Noah's weathered hands receiving her like treasure; his eyes shining.]] And the dove came back... carrying a freshly plucked olive leaf. One green leaf, little ones. After a year in a wooden world, one leaf said everything: somewhere, a tree was growing again. The earth was coming back. God's keeping had held.
PIP: [softly] One little leaf...
NAOMI: Sometimes hope is exactly that size, little lamb. Keep your eyes peeled for leaves.
NARRATOR: [[Dry ground at last: the great door open to green hills; creatures pouring out into light; Noah building a simple altar of thanks, the smoke rising straight and sweet.]] And when the ground was dry, they came out — eight people and every living thing — into a washed new world. And the FIRST thing old Noah built on the new earth... was an altar. Thank You, before anything else.
NARRATOR: [[The sign: a full rainbow breaking across the rain-washed sky, its colors doubling softly in the wet green hills; the family small beneath it, faces lifted.]] And God hung His bow in the clouds — a promise for every generation after: never again such a flood, while the earth endures. Seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, day and night — they shall not cease.
NAOMI: And hear the echo, little ones — Peter wrote, many lifetimes later, that in the ark "a few, that is, eight persons, were brought safely through water." First Peter, chapter three. One door, one refuge, everyone inside kept safe — the ark is one of the old story's great whispers of the rescue still to come. Tuck it in your treasure box; we will hear this whisper again.
ELI: The hammers rang for years and years —
PIP: through all the laughing, all the jeers!
ELI: Then God Himself shut fast the door —
PIP: and eight came through to a washed-new shore!
NAOMI: So, little ones — tonight's one clear thing: obeying God can look strange for a long, long time before it looks wise. Build your boat anyway. Pick up tomorrow's hammer. And when the storm finally comes, you will find the door was never yours to hold — He shuts it Himself.
NAOMI: Quiz time, lamplighters! Who shut the door of the ark? Shout it out!
PIP: GOD did! Himself!
NAOMI: And what did the dove bring back? Shout it out!
ELI: An olive leaf! Hope, exactly that size!
NAOMI: Lamp down low. Say it slow. So your heart will always know.
NAOMI: Noah did everything just as God commanded him. Genesis, chapter six, verse twenty-two.
ELI: Everything, just as God commanded.
PIP: Plank by plank!
ELI: Your word is a lamp for my feet —
PIP: a light for my path so sweet!
ELI: Follow the light!
PIP: Follow the light!
NAOMI: Next time, little ones — the whole world speaks one language and builds one proud tower to reach the sky... and God has a better plan, with a thin golden thread in it. Watch for the thread — it's the first time you'll ever see it.
NARRATOR: [[End card grammar: the lamp close and warm, the night deep behind it.]] Follow for the next lamplight story.
