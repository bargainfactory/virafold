# S1E04 — Two Brothers
# PROMISE: You will learn what crouches at the door of every heart — and that even after the worst choice, mercy leaves a mark.
# REPEATABLE — "Give God your best, not your leftovers."
# ARC — WANT: Cain wants to be accepted without giving his best | NEED: to master the crouching thing instead of feeding it | TURN: God's warning at the door, refused | TEAR-BEAT: the mark of mercy on the one who least deserved it (~72%)
# CAST — CAIN: Physical — a broad strong young farmer, sun-browned, heavy brows, earth-stained tunic, strong hands. Personality — works hard but holds back his best; anger falls on his face (Gen 4:5); God's warning finds him at the door. Aliases — "the older brother", "the farmer".
# CAST — ABEL: Physical — a lean gentle young shepherd, open face, simple light tunic, shepherd's crook, a lamb at his feet. Personality — gives God his first and best (Gen 4:4); quiet, unguarded. Aliases — "the younger brother", "the shepherd".
# CAST — EVE: Physical — a beautiful young woman, warm tan skin, long flowing dark hair, graceful and strong, garments of skins; eyes that have known both Eden and exile. Personality — the mother of all living; fierce hoping. Aliases — "the mother".
# PLACE — THE LAMP ROOM: a cozy ancient night-time home interior, warm stone walls, a woven rug, a single glowing clay oil lamp on a low round stone table at center, soft hearth-glow at one side, deep indigo night through one small arched window.
# NOTE — GOD: never depicted; warm light and the Voice alone.
# NOTE — RESTRAINT: the field is handled off-screen in one hushed narrator line — no blow, no body shown; the episode's weight falls on the warning before and the mercy after.

NARRATOR: [[Palette: wide dawn over hard new country — beautiful, but not Eden. Three-second cold open: the small promise-light from last week drifting low over a stony field at sunrise, leading the eye to a little farmstead with smoke rising.]] Outside the garden, the world was harder. But the promise-light had come along... and life went on. Tonight: the first brothers who ever lived.
NAOMI: Little ones! The stars are out, the night is soft... so tell me — who lights the lamp?
ELI: WE light the lamp!
PIP: Ooooh... watch it glow!
NAOMI: Then gather close, lamplighters — tonight's story is a hard one, told honestly, the way the Bible tells it. But I promise you mercy before the lamp burns low. Sing with me!
ELI: Your word is a lamp for my feet!
PIP: A light for my path so sweet!
ELI: When the road is dark and long —
PIP: Light the lamp and sing along!
NAOMI: Adam and Eve, outside the garden now, were given two sons. And little ones — imagine Eve holding her firstborn, remembering the promise: her offspring would crush the serpent! She must have wondered: is it THIS one?
NARRATOR: [[Split pastoral frame: on one side Cain striding dark furrows behind a wooden plow; on the other Abel on a green hillside, a lamb across his shoulders.]] The boys grew. Cain worked the ground — a farmer, strong as his father. Abel kept the flocks — a shepherd with a gentle hand. Two brothers, two callings, both good.
NARRATOR: [[Two simple stone altars side by side at dusk. On Abel's: the first and finest lamb, offered whole-hearted, warm light resting on it like a hand. On Cain's: a loose pile of ordinary produce, offered sideways, the light thin above it.]] And the day came when both brothers brought God a gift. Abel brought his very best — the firstborn of his flock, the finest he had. Cain brought... some of his crops. Not his first. Not his finest. Just... some.
PIP: What's wrong with vegetables? I LIKE vegetables.
NAOMI: Nothing at all, little lamb — treasure-box time! God never cared about lamb versus lettuce. The Bible says "the Lord looked on Abel AND his offering" — the giver first, the gift second. Abel gave like someone who loved God. Cain gave like someone paying a bill. God reads the heart behind the hands.
NARRATOR: [[Close on Cain: the light resting on his brother's altar reflected small and cold in his eyes; his face falling like a stone.]] And God received Abel's gift with favor... but not Cain's. And Cain burned. His face fell. His fists closed.
NARRATOR: [[The farmstead door at evening; a long low shadow lies across the threshold like something waiting; warm light gathers at Cain's side.]] And God — watch His kindness, little ones — God came to CAIN. To the angry one. With a warning... and a way out.
VOICE(atlas): Why are you angry? If you do what is right, will you not be accepted? But if you do not... sin is crouching at your door. It desires to have you. But you must master it.
ELI: [awe] Crouching... like something ABOUT to pounce?
NAOMI: Exactly like that, Eli. Hear it well, because this warning is for every one of us: anger fed becomes a beast at the door. God told Cain plainly — it wants you, but YOU must master IT. He even told him how: do what is right. The door was still open. The choice was still his.
NARRATOR: [[Hold on the door a quiet beat; then — restraint, distance, dusk: two small figures walking out toward a far field under a lowering sky; the frame does not follow.]] But Cain did not master it. He fed it. He asked his brother out to the field... and there, away from every eye but One, Cain did the terrible thing. And gentle Abel — the shepherd, the whole-hearted giver — was gone.
PIP: [softly] Gone? Just... gone?
NAOMI: [tender] Gone to God, little lamb — who had loved his gift and loved him. The sorrow stays with the ones left. Come, let us see what God does with the one who did it. Watch closely — this is the part that will surprise you.
VOICE(atlas): Where is Abel, your brother?
NARRATOR: [[Cain alone in the empty field, arms crossed over the hollow in his chest; the light before him patient and terrible and kind.]] And Cain said the coldest words yet spoken in the young world: "Am I my brother's keeper?" But nothing is hidden from God. There were consequences — real ones. The ground would fight him. He would wander far from home.
NARRATOR: [[Close, unhurried: a soft radiance touches Cain's brow and leaves a faint luminous sign there; his hard face breaks, not with anger now.]] And then Cain, trembling, said: my punishment is more than I can bear — whoever finds me will repay me! And God... marked him. A mark of MERCY — a protection on the very man whose hands had done the worst. No one would be allowed to harm him.
ELI: [awe] Mercy? For HIM? After THAT?
NAOMI: For him, Eli. After that. Let it sit crooked in your heart a moment — mercy usually does. The Judge of all the earth told the truth about Cain's sin, let the consequences stand... and still wrapped a guard around his life. Justice and mercy, both at full strength. Only God can carry both without dropping either.
ELI: There's a crouching thing at every door —
PIP: it wants a little, then wants more!
ELI: But do what's right, and hold the line —
PIP: and mercy's mark will still be thine!
NAOMI: So, little ones — two clear things tonight, and they belong together. First: give God your BEST, not your leftovers — He reads the heart behind the hands. Second: when anger crouches at your door — and it will — you must master it. Name it out loud. Walk away. Tell someone. Do what is right, and the beast goes hungry.
NAOMI: And if you ever do the thing you cannot take back... remember the mark. Consequences are real. But so is mercy. Nobody is thrown away.
NAOMI: Quiz time, lamplighters! What did God say was crouching at Cain's door? Shout it out!
ELI: SIN! Like a beast — but Cain had to master it!
NAOMI: And what did God put on Cain — a punishment, or a protection? Shout it out!
PIP: A MARK of MERCY! A guard, even for him!
NAOMI: Lamp down low. Say it slow. So your heart will always know.
NAOMI: If you do what is right, will you not be accepted? Genesis, chapter four, verse seven.
ELI: Do what is right.
PIP: And you will be accepted!
ELI: Your word is a lamp for my feet —
PIP: a light for my path so sweet!
ELI: Follow the light!
PIP: Follow the light!
NAOMI: Next time, little ones — the world grows loud and dark, and one old man starts building the biggest boat anyone has ever seen... nowhere near the sea. Bring your hammers.
NARRATOR: [[End card grammar: the lamp close and warm, the night deep behind it.]] Follow for the next lamplight story.
