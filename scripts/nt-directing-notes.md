# New Testament Directing Notes — Season 2: "The Lamp Meets Its Light"
# (Canon draft — companion to series-bible.md and series-outline.md)

## The Great Inversion (the series' structural payoff)

For the whole Old Testament arc, Jesus appeared ONLY in the golden vignette
above the lamp. The New Testament season is the payoff of that discipline:

- **S2E01 (Nativity):** at the moment of birth, the vignette above the lamp
  OPENS — and for the first time in the series, its golden light comes DOWN,
  out of the dream-space, into the historical scene. The children watch the
  light they have seen above the lamp for 38 episodes step into the world.
  This is the single most important shot of the entire series. Lock it.
- From that moment on, **Jesus walks in-scene** as the registered design
  (deep warm tan, sun-browned Middle-Eastern skin, dark wavy hair and beard,
  simple robes, calm kind loving face). The old "never in the OT scene" rule
  retires honorably — He is incarnate now; that was always the point.

## [[Echo]] — the NT's counterpart of the vignette (locked template)

Shadows pointed forward; echoes look back. At fulfillment moments the vignette
above the lamp returns — but now it shows the OLD TESTAMENT shadow, as memory:

- Jesus breaks bread and lifts the cup → echo: Melchizedek's bread and cup.
- "Behold the Lamb of God" at the Jordan → echo: the ram in the thicket.
- "I am the bread of life" → echo: manna on the morning ground.
- Living water → echo: the struck rock.
- "As Moses lifted up the serpent…" → echo: the cross-shaped standard of E23.
- Nathanael under the fig tree → echo: Jacob's ladder ("angels ascending…").
- The Last Supper's "new covenant" → echo: the star field of the covenant.
- Resurrection morning → echo: "Let there be light" (E01's first light).

Rules (mirror the vignette's):
- One Primary echo per episode, drawn ONLY from the Scarlet Thread table.
- Spoken recognition is a child's line where possible: "The ram!" "The bread!"
  — the audience has earned the right to spot it before Naomi confirms.
- Echoes render from the OT episodes' OWN registered imagery (sheets, plates,
  stills) so the memory literally matches what viewers saw.
- Duration = Primary vignette timing (18–30 narration words).
- The 4-note motif returns for echoes — same sound, now meaning "fulfilled."

## The Thread completes

- From the Nativity on, the Thread runs from the lamp INTO Jesus — He visibly
  carries it through every episode.
- At the cross it grows thinner than it has ever been — flickering, exile-thin
  — but NEVER breaks (the one unbreakable rule of the whole series).
- At the resurrection it BLAZES — the brightest Thread state of the series.
- At Pentecost it MULTIPLIES: threads of flame to every believer.
- Final image of the season (and series): a single thread drifts out of the
  lamp toward the camera — toward the child watching. The promise reaches
  the viewer. This is the series' last shareable frame.

## Depicting Jesus (locked)

- **Red-letter discipline:** Jesus speaks ONLY what the Gospels record, in
  faithful child-clear simplification. Paraphrase is allowed; invention never.
  He gets no made-up jokes, no modern idioms, no invented teachings.
- **Voice:** a NEW locked ElevenLabs voice, warm, young-adult male, unhurried
  — distinct from the narrator and from the Father (audition before E01;
  registered in the voice map as "yeshua"). The FATHER keeps the deep echoing
  VOICE treatment (Baptism, Transfiguration: "This is My beloved Son").
- **The Spirit** is shown only in scriptural symbols: the dove at the Jordan,
  wind and tongues of flame at Pentecost. Never a figure.
- Expressions widen from the vignette's three: add joy (wedding at Cana),
  grief (Lazarus — "Jesus wept" gets a held silent close-up), and righteous
  firmness (the temple courts — firm, never raging).

## The Cross and the Resurrection (locked)

- **Dignity grammar, fully in force.** The crucifixion is told with the same
  restraint as every hard OT moment: sorrow, not gore. The cross is seen at
  distance and in silhouette against the darkened sky — deliberately the SAME
  COMPOSITION as E23's cross-shaped standard, and the script says so out
  loud: "Do you remember the serpent lifted up, so that everyone who looked
  would live? Look." John 3:14 closes its own circle.
- Never: nails driven, blood, close-up agony. Always: the mothers' faces, the
  darkness at noon, the Father's silence, the centurion's awe.
- The children are pre-assured — the Akedah rule generalized: Naomi tells
  them before Good Friday begins that Sunday is coming ("this is the saddest
  night of the whole story, little ones — and I promise you, it is not the
  end"). Grief with a held hand, never dread.
- **Resurrection = the series' great light event.** Dawn grammar: the same
  palette move as Moriah (grey-blue flooding to gold) but brighter than any
  episode before it. The stone rolls FROM THE INSIDE with light.

## People handled with care (locked)

- Jesus, Mary, the disciples, the crowds — all visibly Jewish, warm tan,
  Middle-Eastern; the series never permits an us/them framing. Opposition is
  specific named leaders acting from fear and pride — never a people. No
  caricature in face, dress, or voice.
- **Judas:** sorrow, not villainy — a friend who chose wrongly; his grief is
  shown, his end is not.
- **Satan** (temptation): a voice and a shadow at the edge of frame, quoting
  scripture smoothly — never a monster, never costumed horror.
- Demoniacs/illness: fear-free staging; the moment of healing is the shot,
  never the affliction.
- Romans: duty-bound soldiers, not cartoon brutes; Pilate washing his hands
  is the image of avoidance, not a villain beat.

## Production notes

- Same episode skeleton throughout: cold open → lamp liturgy → story → echo
  (if specified) → song → treasure-box → quiz → memory verse → tease.
- The fireside set subtly WARMS across the season (the lamp burns brighter
  scene by scene — the room itself knows).
- JESUS registers in the story-cast registry at S2E01 from the locked design;
  the Transfiguration reuses MOSES' and ELIJAH's registered OT faces — the
  registry's finest hour.
- All OT-season systems apply unchanged: ARC headers, Brain Trust, cast book
  additions (Mary, Joseph of Nazareth, Peter, John, Judas, Pilate, Mary
  Magdalene…), plates (Nazareth, the Jordan, Galilee shore, the upper room,
  Gethsemane, the tomb garden), leitmotifs, QA gate, foley, act grades.
