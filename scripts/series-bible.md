**Rules for the table**
- Primary = full vignette + object bridge + named NT verse.
- Secondary = short vignette or strong spoken call-out.
- Whisper only = verbal recognition only; no vignette.
- One Primary (or carefully chosen Secondary) per episode maximum. Two only on rare, high-value episodes.

## Typology Guardrails

These rules exist so every shadow lands as revelation rather than insertion or decoration.

### What qualifies as a usable type
A passage may receive a vignette or strong spoken treatment only if it meets **all three**:

1. **Historical reality** — The event, person, or institution actually occurred as recorded in the text.
2. **Divine intentionality** — The pattern is not accidental. There is evidence God designed it to point forward.
3. **New Testament confirmation** — Explicit statement, near-explicit quotation, or clear apostolic use as a type.

If any of the three is missing, the maximum treatment is a brief spoken whisper. Never invent a vignette to “make it fit.”

**Pattern-level confirmation** (Joseph): where the NT uses a figure's *pattern* without naming him a type (Acts 7), the entry may be Primary, but the lesson names the pattern honestly ("the Bible loves to tell this shape of story…") and never claims a verse that isn't there.

### Christophany — a different category entirely
A **Christophany** is a passage where the pre-incarnate Christ is arguably *present in the scene itself* (the fourth man in the furnace, the Angel of the LORD). This is not a type and does not use the vignette:
- Depicted **in-scene**, exactly as the text places Him: a light-wrapped figure, face never shown, NEVER the registered Jesus design.
- The narration quotes the text's own words ("like a son of the gods") and Naomi may whisper the traditional recognition — but the show never over-claims what the text leaves in wonder.
- The vignette grammar stays reserved for types; the "never place Jesus inside the OT scene" rule applies to the *registered Jesus*, not to the text's own light-wrapped presence.

### Strength ratings (used in the Scarlet Thread table)
- **Primary**: Full fixed vignette grammar, symbolic object bridge, and clear naming of the NT anchor.
- **Secondary**: Shorter visual treatment or strong spoken recognition with the NT reference.
- **Whisper**: One or two lines of dialogue only. No visual vignette.

### Explicit “Never” list — except where the New Testament itself makes the link
A "never" yields only when a New Testament scripture explicitly draws the picture; the exception is then named in the Scarlet Thread table, never improvised on set.
- Never show crucifixion, cross, or resurrection imagery inside an Old Testament vignette — **except the bronze serpent** (Num 21 / John 3:14–15), where Jesus Himself draws the analogy: "as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that everyone who looks to Him may have eternal life." Treatment for kids: the vignette may mirror the LIFTING — Jesus raised up in golden light, arms open, the lifted pole silhouetted beside Him — never nails, blood, or suffering; the visual rhyme is the lifting and the looking-up-to-live, not the agony.
- Never have Jesus speak dialogue that belongs to the OT characters or advance the OT plot.
- Never imply the OT saints already understood the full New Testament picture.
- Never use the crown of thorns except on clear sacrifice shadows (primarily the ram in the thicket).
- Never place Jesus walking inside the Old Testament scene. He appears only in the golden vignette above the lamp. (Christophanies are the separate category above — the text's own in-scene presence, light-wrapped, never the registered face.)
- Never force a type that fails the three-part test above.

### Writer & director checklist (every shadow episode)
- [ ] Does this entry exist on the Scarlet Thread table?
- [ ] Does the strength rating match the treatment used?
- [ ] Is the symbolic object the clear bridge?
- [ ] Is the exact NT anchor named out loud?
- [ ] Does the vignette follow the fixed grammar (see Visual Grammar section)?
- [ ] Does the story resume cleanly afterward without breaking narrative momentum?

## Fixed Vignette Grammar & Visual Production Rules

The vignette is a ritual. Consistency is what makes it feel like revelation instead of a special effect.

### Core rules
- Jesus appears **only** in the golden dream-vignette positioned above the lamp. He never enters the Old Testament scene.
- Duration: 7–12 seconds. Hard limit.
- The only element that crosses between worlds is the **symbolic object** listed in the Scarlet Thread table.
- Same registered Jesus face, same lighting key (warm, slightly overexposed gold), same three expressions only: compassionate watchfulness, quiet recognition, quiet invitation.
- Jesus does not speak in the vignette.

### Transition sequence (locked)
1. Story reaches the shadow moment.
2. Lamp or “look closer” cue.
3. Symbolic object begins to glow or lift.
4. Vignette opens above the lamp; object is now present with Jesus.
5. NT anchor is spoken clearly while the vignette holds.
6. Object returns or fades; vignette dissolves upward into the lamp light.
7. Immediate return to the continuing OT scene.

### Audio
- A single 4-note motif plays only when the vignette appears. It is never used for any other purpose.

### Visual language
- Color: warm gold with soft falloff. No harsh modern lighting.
- Framing: vignette feels elevated and slightly dream-like relative to the grounded OT scene.
- Restraint: one Primary shadow per episode (two maximum on rare episodes). Overuse dulls the device.

### Production checklist
- [ ] Jesus face matches the permanent registry entry.
- [ ] Duration stays inside 7–12 seconds.
- [ ] Only the approved symbolic object bridges the worlds.
- [ ] NT verse is named clearly.
- [ ] Vignette exits cleanly into the lamp.
- [ ] No prohibited imagery (cross, premature suffering, Jesus walking in the OT world).

## Engine Mapping — how the renderer enforces this bible

These are the concrete hooks in the Virafold engine that make the rules above executable, not aspirational.

### The locked vignette direction template
Every Primary vignette uses THIS direction text, verbatim except the [OBJECT] slot — identical wording is what keeps the vignette face identical, because fireside vignettes are painted from direction text:

```
[[Floating golden dream-vignette glowing in the warm air above the clay lamp: Jesus — deep warm tan, sun-browned Middle-Eastern skin, dark wavy hair and beard, simple cream robes, a calm kind loving face — holding/with [OBJECT], soft golden radiance all around Him; the fireside cast gaze up at it in wonder.]]
```
- Crown of thorns: add "wearing a simple woven crown of thorns" ONLY on sacrifice shadows (the ram; later the Passover lamb and Day of Atonement).
- Duration is enforced as narration length: the carrying line is 18–30 spoken words (≈ 7–12 seconds at narration pace).

### Other mappings
- **Registry**: JESUS may be registered in story_cast for story-beat recalls, but vignette consistency rides the template above; the registered design text and the template must never drift apart.
- **The 4-note motif**: to be composed once via ElevenLabs Music (like the signature jingle) and wired as a song-slot overlay on vignette beats — future `ensureFilmSongs` hook, not yet built.
- **Promise Thread** (gold filament with faint crimson edge) and **The Register** (growing constellation): written as `[[...]]` directions at covenant moments; the Register doubles as a season end-card asset updated per episode.
- **Era hand-offs** (Patriarchs→Exodus, Wilderness→Land, Kingdom→Exile): cold-open recap episodes using registry faces in flashback beats — the recall bench makes these automatic.
- **Parent/teacher layer**: each Primary episode's study notes (already compiled into the clip's notes field at render) carry the OT+NT texts, one sentence on why the type is strong, and one discussion question building on prior shadows.
- **Script lint** (future): an episode flagged as a shadow episode must contain exactly one vignette template block and its named NT anchor in spoken text.
