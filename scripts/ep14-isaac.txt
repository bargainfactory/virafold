# ========== CAST PROFILES — scripture-grounded production notes (never spoken) ==========
# CAST — ISAAC: Physical — a broad-shouldered, calm man of about seventy-five, short grey-streaked beard, kind unhurried eyes, sand-colored herdsman's robes with a plain leather belt and a long staff; the strong frame of a man who digs wells with his servants (Gen 26:18-22). Personality — a quiet evening soul who walks out to meditate in the fields at dusk (Gen 24:63); prays and waits rather than grabs (Gen 25:21); steps back from quarrels he could win and digs again (Gen 26:22); honors his father's works by restoring them (Gen 26:18); makes feasts for former opponents (Gen 26:30). Aliases — "the son of Abraham", "her husband", "the quiet man", "the digger of wells".
# CAST — REBEKAH: Physical — a strikingly beautiful young woman (Gen 24:16) when she first appears at the well, dark braided hair under a light head-scarf, a large clay water-jar carried on her shoulder, simple graceful robes in dusk-rose and cream; in later scenes a dignified wife of about seventy with silver-streaked braids, the same bright decisive eyes. Personality — swift, generous hospitality (Gen 24:18-20 — she watered ten thirsty camels unasked); brave enough to leave home on a day's notice ("I will go" — Gen 24:58); decisive and quick-witted. Aliases — "the mother", "his wife", "the girl at the well".
# CAST — ABIMELECH: Physical — a Philistine king of Gerar, strong build, oiled dark beard, fine sea-colored robes with bronze trim and a thin gold circlet; arrives flanked by his advisor and his army commander (Gen 26:26). Personality — proud and wary, but honest enough to recognize the Lord's blessing when he sees it and to ask for peace (Gen 26:28-29). Aliases — "the king".
# GROUP — ISAAC'S HERDSMEN: patient weathered shepherds in earth-tone tunics and head-wraps, carrying digging staves and water-skins, moving as one calm household.
# GROUP — THE HERDSMEN OF GERAR: rival shepherds in dusty grey-and-ochre wraps, quick-tempered faces, crowding and pointing as a pack (Gen 26:20-21).
# PROP — THE WELLS: stone-rimmed desert wells, each opening wider than a man's outstretched arms, cool dark water far below; the quarrelled wells named Esek and Sitnah, the peaceful one Rehoboth (Gen 26:20-22).
# PROP — THE WATER-JAR: Rebekah's large round clay jar with two handles, carried on the shoulder — the same jar in every well scene.
# NOTE — GOD: never depicted as a person or figure; His presence is warm light and the Voice alone (Gen 26:24, appearing by night).

NARRATOR: [[Two crowds of herdsmen face each other across the well, shouting, dust rising around their sandals; between them one broad calm man in sand-colored robes.]] And see two crowds of herdsmen facing each other across one well, shouting, fists raised — and between them, one quiet man who could have fought... and chose, again and again, something braver.
NARRATOR: This is the story of Isaac — the boy from the mountain, now grown — the man who dug wells instead of wounds.
NAOMI: Little ones! The stars are out, the night is soft... so tell me — who lights the lamp?
ELI: WE light the lamp!
PIP: Ooooh... watch it glow!
NAOMI: Then gather close, lamplighters — because tonight's story continues last week's! Do you remember the boy on the mountain? The ram caught in the thicket? Tonight that boy is all grown up. Sing it with me!
ELI: Your word is a lamp for my feet!
PIP: A light for my path so sweet!
ELI: When the road is dark and long —
PIP: Light the lamp and sing along!
NAOMI: Now, a little remembering, because every story grows out of the one before it. Abraham trusted God up the mountain, and God provided the ram — and God promised that Abraham's family would become as many as the stars. Isaac — the boy named "he laughs" — was the first star of that promise.
PIP: Isaac the baby! The one Sarah laughed about!
NAOMI: The very one, little lamb. And here is something for your Bible-study treasure box: in those days, in that land, a family lived or died by two things — its FLOCKS and its WELLS. No well, no water. No water, no sheep, no garden, no life. Keep that in your pocket; tonight's whole story turns on it.
NARRATOR: Now when Isaac was grown, old Abraham wanted one more thing before his long life ended: a kind, brave wife for his son — from his own people, far away to the north.
NARRATOR: [[Wide desert shot: a servant's small caravan of ten camels arriving at a town well at evening, women coming out with jars on their shoulders.]] So Abraham's most trusted servant traveled far, with ten thirsty camels, and stopped at a town well at evening — the hour when the young women came out to draw water.
NAOMI: And listen to how the servant chose, little ones — he didn't look for the prettiest robes or the richest family. He PRAYED. He asked God: let the right girl be the one who offers water not just to me... but to all my camels too.
ELI: What's so special about camels drinking?
NAOMI: Eli — one thirsty camel can drink more than twenty of your bath-tubs! Ten camels means HUNDREDS of trips down into that well with one clay jar. He was asking God to show him a heart that serves until the work is DONE.
NARRATOR: [[Evening golden light at the well; Rebekah with her clay jar on her shoulder, smiling, pouring water into a stone trough while camels drink; the servant watching in wonder.]] And before he had finished praying, a young woman named Rebekah came to the well — and when he asked her for a sip, she smiled and said: drink — and I will draw water for your camels too, until they have finished.
NARRATOR: Jar after jar after jar she poured, her braids swinging, until every last camel had finished drinking. And the servant watched in silent wonder — for this was the very sign he had prayed for.
PIP: She watered ALL TEN camels? That's... that's SO much up-and-down!
NAOMI: That is what a generous heart looks like when nobody is making it generous, little lamb. And when they asked Rebekah: will you leave your home and go to marry Isaac? — she gave one of the bravest short answers in the whole Bible. Two words. "I will go."
NARRATOR: [[Dusk field; Isaac walking alone in the evening light, looking up to see the camel caravan approaching on the horizon.]] And Isaac — the quiet man who walked in the fields at evening to think and to pray — looked up one dusk and saw the camels coming. And Rebekah became his wife, and he loved her. And in her, old Abraham saw God's promise take its next step before his long, full life came to its end.
NAOMI: So the mountain boy now had the well girl beside him. Remember them both — the trusting one and the generous one. Now comes the testing time.
NARRATOR: Years later, a famine came to the land — the rain forgot to fall, and the fields forgot to grow.
ELI: Famine means no food, doesn't it?
NAOMI: It does, Eli. And here's your treasure-box fact: in a famine, everyone in that land packed up and went DOWN to Egypt, where the great river never ran dry. That was simply what one did. Watch what God tells Isaac instead.
NARRATOR: So Isaac moved his tents toward Gerar, in the land of king Abimelech, on the road that led down to Egypt. And God spoke to him there, the same way He had spoken to his father.
VOICE(atlas): Do not go down to Egypt. Stay in this land, and I will be with you and bless you — for I will keep the promise I made to Abraham your father.
NARRATOR: [[Wide shot: Isaac sowing seed by hand across a dusty field under a hard bright sky, his herdsmen behind him.]] And Isaac did the strangest thing a man can do in a famine: he stayed. And he planted seed in that dry land — and God blessed him so richly that his harvest came back a hundredfold, and his flocks spread across the hills like clouds on the grass.
NAOMI: A hundredfold, little ones — plant one basket, harvest a hundred — in a FAMINE year. Genesis chapter twenty-six says it plainly: the Lord blessed him. Obedience in a dry season grows the greenest fields.
NARRATOR: But watch the faces of his neighbors as his flocks grow: tight mouths, folded arms, narrowed eyes. The people of Gerar grew jealous.
NAOMI: Jealousy is a weed, little ones. It grows fastest in a dry season — when everyone is afraid there isn't enough to go around.
NARRATOR: And the jealous neighbors did a mean and wasteful thing: they crept out and filled Isaac's wells with earth — the very wells his father Abraham had dug long ago, stopped up, one by one.
PIP: They put DIRT in the WATER? In a FAMINE?
ELI: That's not just mean. That's mean to EVERYBODY. Even to themselves!
NAOMI: That is what spite does, little ones — it poisons the well it drinks from. And then King Abimelech himself came to Isaac and said: you have grown too mighty for us. Go away.
NARRATOR: [[Isaac standing before the king, calm, strong hands at his sides; behind him his whole household waits.]] Now watch Isaac, standing before the king. He had men enough to argue. He had wealth enough to fight.
NARRATOR: And Isaac bowed his head... and moved his tents away down the valley, without one angry word.
ELI: He just... LEFT? But the wells were HIS!
NAOMI: They were, Eli. And here is tonight's riddle, so hold it carefully: sometimes the strongest man in the valley is the one who walks away from the fight he could have won.
NARRATOR: [[Isaac's herdsmen hauling baskets of earth up out of an old well, cool water winking below.]] In the valley, Isaac's servants dug again the wells of his father Abraham, clearing out the earth, until the cool water winked up at the sky once more. And Isaac gave each well back its father's old name — a son keeping his father's work alive.
NARRATOR: And they dug a brand-new well — the opening wider than a man's arms, the water sweet and bright. And the herdsmen of Gerar came swarming over the hill shouting: the water is ours!
NARRATOR: Isaac named that well Esek — which means "argument" — and he did not fight. He moved on, and dug another.
NARRATOR: And the herdsmen came and quarreled over THAT one too. So he named it Sitnah — "opposition" — and moved on again, and dug another.
PIP: Again?! Naomi, how many times must a person dig?
NAOMI: As many times as peace takes, little lamb. Watch what happens to the diggers — and watch what happens to the quarrelers. One of them is building something. The other is only following, with empty hands.
ELI: Dig a well, dig it deep —
PIP: some things are better to give than keep!
ELI: They can take the water, take the ground —
PIP: they can't take the peace we've found!
NARRATOR: [[Wide quiet valley at golden hour; one new well, no crowds anywhere, Isaac standing straight beside it with his face to the sky.]] And then Isaac moved on and dug once more — and this time, no one came. No shouting. No fists. Only the wide quiet valley, and water enough for everyone.
ISAAC(orion): Rehoboth! The Lord has made room for us, and we will flourish in the land!
NARRATOR: Rehoboth — "wide open spaces." The well of room-enough. And that very night, the Voice came to Isaac under the stars.
VOICE(atlas): I am the God of your father Abraham. Do not be afraid, for I am with you, and I will bless you.
NAOMI: Do you hear it, little ones? The same promise, handed down like a lamp from father to son. God introduces Himself as "the God of your father" — because His faithfulness doesn't start over with each new person. It carries FORWARD.
NARRATOR: [[The king and two robed officials walking into Isaac's camp; Isaac rising to meet them.]] And then, look up the road: King Abimelech himself came walking into Isaac's camp, with his advisor and the commander of his army.
ISAAC(orion): Why have you come to me — you, who sent me away?
ABIMELECH(rex): We have seen plainly that the Lord is with you. Let there be peace between us. Let us make a promise to one another.
NARRATOR: And Isaac — the man they had wronged, the man whose wells they had stopped — did not turn them away. He made them a feast, and they ate and drank together, and in the morning they made their promises of peace.
NAOMI: Treasure-box time! In those days, when two households made peace, they sealed it with a shared MEAL — eating together meant "my family and your family are safe with each other now." So when Isaac cooked a feast for the very people who had wronged him... that supper WAS the peace treaty.
ELI: So the man who would not fight for his wells won... without fighting at all?
NAOMI: He won something no fight ever wins, Eli: his enemies came to HIM, asking for his peace. Gentleness conquered the whole valley without one raised fist.
NARRATOR: And that same day — that very same day — Isaac's servants came running from the newest well with news.
SERVANT(leo): We have found water!
NAOMI: God's little wink at the end of the story, little ones. The day peace was sealed, the ground itself opened up with more. There is always enough in God's valley — it is only fear that makes the land feel small.
NARRATOR: [[Warm tent interior at night; Rebekah cradling newborn TWIN babies, Isaac leaning close in wonder.]] And God gave Isaac and Rebekah one more gift — after their own long years of praying and waiting: TWINS. Two baby boys, wrestling already in their mother's arms. The first came out red and fuzzy all over, so they called him Esau — which means "hairy." The second came out gripping his brother's heel, so they called him Jacob — "the heel-grabber."
PIP: They named the baby HAIRY? And the other one HEEL-GRABBER?
NAOMI: They did, little lamb — in that land, a name told the truth about you, and those two names told the whole story that was coming. Remember that grip. Next week, the heel-grabbing baby gets a whole story of his own — and oh, what a story it is.
NAOMI: So, little ones. Peace. Isaac teaches the lesson most grown-ups still struggle with: being right about the well matters less than being righteous beside it. Anyone can grab. It takes a giant heart to let go and dig again.

Wisdom is knowing which moment you are in. But hear me: nobody ever lay awake at night regretting that they made a peace.
