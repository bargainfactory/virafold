# S1E03 — The First Promise
# PROMISE: On the saddest day there ever was, you will hear God make the first promise — and watch its light refuse to go out.
# REPEATABLE — "The light stayed on."
# ARC — WANT: Eli wants the sad part to not be true | NEED: to learn that God answers our worst day with a promise, not abandonment | TURN: Genesis 3:15 spoken into the ruins | TEAR-BEAT: they leave the garden, but the promise-light goes WITH them (~72%)
# CAST — ADAM: Physical — a strong young man in his first days, warm deep-tan skin, thick dark curly hair and short beard, simple garment of woven leaves, bright unguarded eyes; after the fall, simple garments of skins (Gen 3:21). Personality — wonder-filled namer; after the fall carries a quieter, humbled hope. Aliases — "the man", "the first man".
# CAST — EVE: Physical — a beautiful young woman, warm tan skin, long flowing dark hair, graceful and strong, garment of woven leaves, then skins; eyes quick with delight, then wet with the first tears. Personality — curious and brave-hearted; the mother of all living (Gen 3:20). Aliases — "the woman", "the mother of all living".
# CAST — THE SERPENT: Physical — an elegant gleaming serpent of burnished copper-gold scales moving upright among branches, beautiful and subtly wrong, never horror. Personality — smooth, flattering, question-twisting (Gen 3:1 — "Did God really say…?"). Aliases — "the serpent", "the snake".
# PLACE — THE LAMP ROOM: a cozy ancient night-time home interior, warm stone walls, a woven rug, a single glowing clay oil lamp on a low round stone table at center, soft hearth-glow at one side, deep indigo night through one small arched window.
# PLACE — THE GARDEN OF EDEN: a paradise garden of impossible green, a crystal river braiding through flowering meadows, fruit trees heavy with every color, soft golden light always at the hour before evening, two great trees at its heart.
# NOTE — GOD: never depicted; warm light and the Voice alone.
# NOTE — RESTRAINT: the taking of the fruit is shown in aftermath, never relished; shame is hiding and lowered eyes, never nakedness played for the camera; the children are pre-assured that God's promise is coming before the sorrow lands.

NARRATOR: [[Palette: the garden's gold, cooled by one degree. Three-second cold open: the solemn tree fills the frame — and the copper-gold serpent slides slowly down its trunk toward the camera, beautiful and wrong, eyes like polished coins.]] Do you remember the gleam in the tree? Tonight... it speaks.
NAOMI: Little ones! The stars are out, the night is soft... so tell me — who lights the lamp?
ELI: WE light the lamp!
PIP: Ooooh... watch it glow!
NAOMI: Then gather close, lamplighters — and come very close tonight, because this is the saddest story we will ever tell by this lamp... and inside it, the brightest promise. Sing it with me first, so your flames are tall!
ELI: Your word is a lamp for my feet!
PIP: A light for my path so sweet!
ELI: When the road is dark and long —
PIP: Light the lamp and sing along!
NAOMI: Before we begin, little ones, hold this with both hands: tonight something precious breaks. But GOD does not break, and He does not leave — and before the story ends, He will make a promise that every lamp in every story after this one is lit by. Ready? Be brave with me.
NARRATOR: [[The garden at the solemn tree; Eve near its branches; the serpent coiled at eye level, head swaying gently, courteous as a merchant.]] One day, by the one forbidden tree, the serpent spoke to the woman — the smoothest voice in all the garden.
THE SERPENT(sal): Did God... REALLY say you must not eat from any tree in the garden?
NAOMI: Do you hear the trick, little ones? God had said no such thing — one tree, out of a whole world of yes. The enemy's oldest tool is a twisted question: making God's one loving "no" sound like a thousand mean ones.
THE SERPENT(sal): You will not surely die. God knows that when you eat of it, your eyes will be opened... and you will be like God.
ELI: But they were ALREADY like God! Made in His image — you said so!
NAOMI: [softly] Well remembered, Eli. The serpent offered them the one thing they already had... for the price of the one thing they'd been asked to keep. That is how lies work: they sell you your own shoes.
NARRATOR: [[Aftermath, not act: two fruit stems on the grass; Adam and Eve back to back among the trees, hands full of sewn fig leaves, eyes lowered; the garden's gold gone grey-green around them.]] And the woman took, and ate, and gave to her husband, and he ate. And at once — like a lamp blown out — something changed. They felt bare. They felt afraid. They hid.
PIP: [softly] Hid? From the evening walk?
NARRATOR: [[Palette: dusk without gold. The familiar warm brightness moves through the trees; the leaves bow as it passes; two figures crouch behind the hedge.]] And in the cool of the day, God came walking in the garden, as He always did. And the Voice called — not with thunder... with sorrow.
VOICE(atlas): Where are you?
NAOMI: [tender] Little ones — He KNEW where they were. He always knows. That question wasn't a search. It was a door held open: come out, tell Me, let us face it together. He asks us the same, every time we hide.
NARRATOR: [[Adam and Eve stepping out, leaf-clad, heads low; honest sorrow on every face; the light before them steady and unflinching.]] And it all came out — the serpent, the fruit, the blame passed hand to hand. And God grieved. But listen now, lamplighters, listen with your whole flame — because God turned to the serpent, and spoke the words this entire series is made of.
VOICE(atlas): I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers. He shall crush your head... and you shall bruise his heel.
NARRATOR: [[From the spoken words a small intense LIGHT kindles in the air — a promise made visible, a warm golden spark that does not flicker; the ruined garden reflects in it, whole.]] A promise — the first promise! One day, from this very family, a Son would come. The serpent would wound Him... but HE would crush the serpent forever.
NAOMI: [awe] And where was that promise pointing, little ones? Lean close... and watch the lamp. [[Floating golden dream-vignette glowing in the warm air above the clay lamp: Jesus — deep warm tan, sun-browned Middle-Eastern skin, long dark wavy hair and beard, simple cream robes, a calm kind loving face — holding/with the small bright promise-light cupped gently in His hands, the crushed serpent's shadow fading beneath His feet, soft golden radiance all around Him; the fireside cast gaze up at it in wonder.]]
NAOMI: There. Him. Many lifetimes before Bethlehem, on the worst day there ever was, God was already telling us about His Son. Paul wrote it plainly: "The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet." Romans, chapter sixteen, verse twenty. The first promise... and He kept it.
PIP: [awe] So the sad day... had a promise hiding inside it?
NAOMI: The sad day is WHERE the promise was born, little lamb. Write that where you keep your treasures.
NARRATOR: [[God's tenderness in one image: two garments of soft skins laid over a stone, warm light resting on them.]] And God — even then, even that day — made clothes for His children with His own hands, garments of skins, and dressed them like a father dressing little ones for a cold morning.
NARRATOR: [[Palette: dusk into deep blue. Wide, sorrowful, beautiful: Adam and Eve walking out through the garden gate, hand in hand; ahead of them, low in the darkening air, the small promise-light goes BEFORE them like a lantern; behind, the gate closes in radiance.]] They could not stay in Eden — not with the broken thing unhealed. So they went out into the wide cold world. But look, little ones. Look what went with them. The promise-light. Out of the garden, into the dark, the light... stayed... on.
ELI: [softly] It went WITH them.
NAOMI: It always does, Eli. It always does.
ELI: The gate swung shut, the gold turned grey —
PIP: but the little light led all the way!
ELI: The serpent grinned, but heard it too —
PIP: "Someone is coming... to crush you!"
NAOMI: So, little ones — tonight's one clear thing: when we break what is precious, God does not throw us away. He grieves, He tells the truth, He covers us... and He PROMISES. Every lamp we light in every story from now on is this little promise-light, passed hand to hand, growing — until the night it comes down and stays.
NAOMI: Quiz time, lamplighters! What did God call out in the garden when they hid? Shout it out!
PIP: "WHERE ARE YOU?" — a door, not a search!
NAOMI: And the first promise — who will crush the serpent's head? Shout it out!
ELI: HER SON! The One who was coming — and He DID!
NAOMI: Lamp down low. Say it slow. So your heart will always know.
NAOMI: The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet. Romans, chapter sixteen, verse twenty.
ELI: The God of peace.
PIP: Under your feet!
ELI: Your word is a lamp for my feet —
PIP: a light for my path so sweet!
ELI: Follow the light!
PIP: Follow the light!
NAOMI: Next time, little ones — two brothers bring two gifts, and one hard lesson about the crouching thing at the door... and the mercy that marks even the one who falls. Hold each other tight.
NARRATOR: [[End card grammar: the lamp close and warm, the night deep behind it.]] Follow for the next lamplight story.
